Drop-off, in detail

Five ways to hand something over.

Not a single counter — a shop can take something in five different ways, depending on what you're bringing and how much distance you want from the sale.

Available around the clock

Locker

A rented compartment at the shop, open day and night — no need to time your visit to opening hours. The buyer and seller never have to meet.

Half an hour, reserved

Table & coffee

Book a table and a slot. Meet the buyer face to face, over a coffee, to sell two or three items in one sitting — then it's someone else's turn.

Vitrine

Rent a section of the shop window. Your items are displayed, not shelved — seen from the street before they're seen online.

Comptoir

Sell it straight to the shop, on the spot — like a pawn counter. They set the price, you get the cash immediately.

Reception

Leave it at the counter for a euro or two. Someone else comes to collect it later — you never cross paths, no evaluation, no small talk.

Why "Curb"

A curb is a physical edge — and, as a verb, a restraint. Both meanings sit inside the same word: a marketplace that gives an unstructured, informal trade a real boundary.

The name has a direct precedent. In 19th-century New York, trading that couldn't get a seat on the stock exchange floor happened on the curb outside — literally, brokers on the sidewalk. That informal market eventually got walls, rules, and a new name: the American Stock Exchange. Same shift Curb is betting on for secondhand trade.

Your shop, more foot traffic.
